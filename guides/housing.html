Housing

Finding a place to live in Ireland — the honest guide

← Back to Resources

The Reality

Let's be upfront: Ireland's rental market is extremely competitive, especially in Dublin. Demand far exceeds supply. But it's not impossible — SA people are resourceful, and community connections help.

Where to Search

  • Daft.ie — the main rental platform in Ireland. Set up alerts immediately.
  • Rent.ie — another option, fewer listings but less competition per listing
  • Facebook Groups — "Dublin Rooms to Rent", "Accommodation in Dublin" etc.
  • Community WhatsApp — SA community members often share listings before they go public
SA Tip: Respond to listings within MINUTES of posting. The good ones get 100+ enquiries in an hour. Have your documents ready in a PDF to attach immediately: employer letter, reference from previous landlord, photo ID.

What to Expect Pricewise (2025)

  • Dublin city: €1,800 - €2,500/month for a 1-bed apartment
  • Dublin suburbs: €1,500 - €2,000/month
  • Cork/Galway: €1,200 - €1,800/month
  • Smaller cities: €900 - €1,400/month
  • Room share: €700 - €1,200/month in Dublin

Understanding Irish Leases

  • Standard lease: 12 months (some are 6 months)
  • Deposit: usually 1 month's rent
  • Rent is paid monthly in advance
  • Rent increases are capped at 2% per year in Rent Pressure Zones (most cities)
  • Landlords must register with the RTB (Residential Tenancies Board)
  • After 6 months, you have Part 4 tenancy rights — much stronger protection

Your Rights as a Tenant

Irish tenant protections are strong. Key ones:

  • Landlord must give notice to end tenancy (28 days to 224 days depending on tenancy length)
  • Deposit must be returned within agreed time, with itemised deductions only
  • Landlord must maintain the property in a habitable state
  • You can't be evicted for making a complaint to the RTB
  • Disputes go to the RTB — free mediation and adjudication

Areas to Consider

Dublin

  • Sandyford/Dundrum — popular with SA families, good schools, Luas access
  • Swords/Malahide — north Dublin, more space, near the airport
  • Tallaght/Citywest — more affordable, Luas red line
  • Lucan/Celbridge — suburban, family-friendly, drive to city

Outside Dublin

  • Cork — second city, growing tech hub, more affordable
  • Galway — vibrant, smaller, strong community feel
  • Limerick — underrated, very affordable, growing
  • Waterford — quiet, affordable, coastal
SA Tip: Consider commuter towns. Places like Navan, Maynooth, or Naas are 30-50 min from Dublin but significantly cheaper. Many SA families settle there for space and value.
