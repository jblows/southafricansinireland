Banking & Finance

Managing your money between South Africa and Ireland

← Back to Resources

Opening an Irish Bank Account

You'll need an Irish bank account to receive your salary. Options:

  • Bank of Ireland — largest branch network, solid online banking
  • AIB — good app, widely used
  • Permanent TSB — competitive fees
  • Revolut — digital-only, instant setup with passport, great for day-to-day (Irish IBAN available)
  • N26 — another digital option with Irish/German IBAN
SA Tip: Open a Revolut account the day you arrive — it gives you an Irish IBAN instantly (useful for salary payments and direct debits). Then apply for a traditional bank account alongside — these take 1-2 weeks to set up.

Documents Needed for Traditional Banks

  • Passport or national ID
  • Proof of address (utility bill, employer letter, or Revenue correspondence)
  • PPS number (some banks accept without, but most want it)
  • Employment letter or contract

The catch-22: you need proof of address to open a bank account, but you need a bank account to rent. Solution: use your employer's address initially, or a letter from whoever you're staying with.

Sending Money to South Africa

You'll likely want to send money home regularly. Options ranked by value:

  • Wise (formerly TransferWise) — best rates, transparent fees, fast (1-2 days). Most SA expats use this.
  • Revolut — competitive rates for transfers, easy to use
  • CurrencyFair — Irish company, good rates, peer-to-peer matching
  • Western Union / MoneyGram — convenient but expensive. Avoid for regular transfers.
  • Bank transfer (SWIFT) — worst rates, highest fees. Never use for ZAR transfers.
SA Tip: Set up rate alerts on Wise. The ZAR fluctuates significantly — timing your transfers around a good rate can save you thousands of rand per year. Some community members share rate alerts in the WhatsApp group.

Tax Residency & Revenue

If you live in Ireland 183+ days per year, you're tax resident here.

  • Register with Revenue (revenue.ie) to get your tax credits
  • PAYE system: tax deducted from salary automatically
  • Standard rate: 20% on first €42,000, 40% on the rest
  • Plus USC (Universal Social Charge): 0.5% - 8% depending on income
  • Plus PRSI: 4% (this funds social insurance — pension, maternity, jobseeker's)
  • Effective tax rate for €60,000 salary: ~35%

SARS Obligations While Abroad

South Africa taxes on worldwide income unless you formally emigrate for tax purposes.

  • If you haven't financially emigrated: You must still file SA tax returns. The first R1.25 million earned abroad is exempt, then 40% inclusion rate on the rest.
  • Financial emigration (ceasing tax residency): Notify SARS, complete the process through an authorised dealer (your SA bank). This triggers exit tax on retirement funds.
  • Double Taxation Agreement: Ireland and SA have a DTA — you won't pay full tax in both countries, but you need to manage it.
  • Get specialist advice — SA tax for expats is complex. See our directory for SA tax consultants.
SA Tip: Don't ignore SARS. Many expats think "out of sight, out of mind" but SARS is actively pursuing non-compliant expats. Get proper advice, even if it costs a bit upfront — it saves major headaches later.

Retirement & Savings

  • Irish employer pensions: many companies offer matching contributions (take it — it's free money)
  • State pension: qualify after 10+ years of PRSI contributions (520 weeks). Worth ~€14,000/year at full rate.
  • SA retirement funds: you can leave them in SA (growing in ZAR) or withdraw on emigration (taxed)
  • Consider speaking to a financial advisor who understands both jurisdictions
